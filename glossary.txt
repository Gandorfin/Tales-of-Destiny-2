GLOSSARY — Tales of Destiny 2

Researched from published game material (wikis, official sites, series
cross-references) since Tales of Destiny 2 itself has no official English release.
Treat "Confirmed" entries as safe to lock in; treat "Needs team decision" entries as
open questions — pick one and apply it consistently everywhere. Extend this list as
translators encounter new names; anything added here overrides individual judgment
calls going forward.

STYLE NOTE: the Tales series' official English localizations consistently spell
special skills "arte" (not "art") and use "Mystic Arte" for a character's ultimate
attack (Japanese 奥義, hi-ougi). Keep both conventions for consistency with the rest of
the series.

DISAMBIGUATION WARNING: if anyone on the team searches for official English material on
"Tales of Destiny II," they will likely land on the wrong game. The North American PS2
release called Tales of Destiny II (Roman numeral) is actually a rebrand of Tales of
Eternia — Namco renamed it because Mattel holds the "Eternia" trademark (He-Man toys)
in North America. It shares no characters, story, or terminology with this game. This
project is translating the Japan-only Tales of Destiny 2 (Arabic numeral), the true
sequel to the first Tales of Destiny. Don't import terms from Eternia material.

== MINOR ANTAGONISTS & DEMONOLOGY-NAMED BOSSES ==

  Minor antagonist, brief obstacle at Heidelberg Castle

  Optional final boss of the Aqua Labyrinth EX Dungeon

NAMING CONVENTION: several Destiny 2 antagonists and bosses (Barbatos, Sabnock, and
reportedly others including Dantalion, Gaap, Ose, Vassago, Buer, Glasya-Labolas,
Forneus, Halphas, and Vepar) take their names from the Ars Goetia / Lesser Key of
Solomon, a 17th-century demonology grimoire. If translators encounter one of these
names, use the name's standard historical English spelling (as used in English
editions of the grimoire) rather than re-transliterating from the katakana.
