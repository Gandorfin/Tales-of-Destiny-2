CHARACTER VOICE GUIDE — Tales of Destiny 2

Treat this as a first pass based on published character material, not a final locked
guide — have someone on the team who has played the game sanity-check it, especially
the tone shifts noted for Reala and Judas, before translators lean on it heavily.

Kyle Dunamis (protagonist)
Loud, earnest, hot-blooded, quick to shout. Simple, direct sentences over anything
flowery. Genuinely bad at picking up on sarcasm or dry humor from others — when he
misses a joke, let that land as a beat of confusion rather than having him "get it" in
translation. His outbursts of Reala's name are a running gag; stretched/caps spelling
("REEEALLLAAA!") is appropriate where the Japanese does similar vocal stretching.

Reala
Soft-spoken and formal, apologizes frequently early in the game ("Sorry, I didn't mean
to—", "Forgive me for—"). Tentative, hedging phrasing at first. As her arc progresses
she becomes noticeably more direct and confident — let her sentences get shorter and
surer over the course of the script rather than keeping one register throughout.
Polite, slightly otherworldly diction even once she opens up; light on contractions
early, more natural with them later.

Loni Dunamis
Big-brother energy toward Kyle: teasing, snarky, occasionally landing as clumsy rather
than clever. Overconfident about his own charm with women — his flirting should read
as try-hard, not smooth, since the joke is that he's not as good at it as he thinks.
Comfortable, punchy, casual phrasing; good candidate for natural English idiom/slang in
the Tales localization tradition.

Judas (masked ally; secretly Lion Magnus, a character from the first game)
Deadpan and cynical, short clipped sentences, sarcasm as his default register. Rarely
raises his voice in ordinary dialogue — the one deliberate exception is his named
special-attack incantation, which should stay grandiose and theatrical, since that's a
stylistic outlier even for him. Avoid warming up his phrasing; he holds people at arm's
length in how he talks even when he's actually helping them.

Nanaly Fletcher
Blunt, tough, quick to snap, especially if teased about being unfeminine or "a
tomboy" — that's a hard trigger for her. Classic tsundere pattern: sharp, insulting
surface language masking real warmth underneath, particularly toward Reala, who she's
protective of. Keep her insults sharp-edged but never actually mean-spirited underneath.

Harold Berselius
Bubbly and theatrical, unsettlingly cheerful about dark subject matter (dissection,
experiments, poisoning party members "for science"). The comedy is in the contrast, so
keep her tone light and singsong regardless of how disturbing the content is — don't
let the translation soften the content to match a nicer tone, and don't let the tone
darken to match the content. Comfortable mixing scientific vocabulary into casual
speech; occasional English-loanword flourishes fit her established character.

Barbatos Goetia (antagonist)
Big, hammy, deliberately over-the-top delivery — he's a villain performing his own
menace. Pre-attack lines/incantations should stay theatrical and exclamatory, matching
the source's pattern of dramatic declarations before spells. His grievance about being
erased from history / denied recognition as a hero surfaces even mid-threat — it's his
core obsession, not incidental color.

Elraine (antagonist)
Composed, serene, formally polite at all times, never raises her voice even when saying
something ruthless. The horror should come through in the content of her lines, not the
delivery — avoid "evil villain" line reads; her menace is the contrast between pleasant
tone and monstrous substance.

Minor/context note: Judas's true identity (Lion Magnus) and his "older than he looks"
age ambiguity are not meant to be a secret from the player, so translated dialogue
doesn't need to dance around it once the story reveals it.
